## 🎯 Pedagogical Blueprint & Objective
Train students to explicitly compare topologies (e.g. proving the Lower Limit / Sorgenfrey line $\mathbb{R}_\ell$ with basis $[a, b)$ is strictly finer than the standard Euclidean topology $\mathbb{R}_{\text{std}}$), verify basis conditions for candidate collections, and determine countability axioms.

## 🤖 LLM Prompt Generation Rules & Directives

1. **Slide 1 (`theory`):** State the worked problem: Compare the standard topology $\mathcal{T}_{\text{std}}$ on $\mathbb{R}$ (basis $\mathcal{B} = \{(a, b)\}$) with the Lower Limit topology $\mathcal{T}_\ell$ (basis $\mathcal{B}_\ell = \{[a, b)\}$). Prove that $\mathcal{T}_{\text{std}} \subsetneq \mathcal{T}_\ell$.
2. **Slide 2 (`concept_pieces`):** Deconstruct the 4 steps: (1) let $(a, b) \in \mathcal{B}$ and $x \in (a, b)$, (2) choose $[x, b) \in \mathcal{B}_\ell$; then $x \in [x, b) \subseteq (a, b)$, proving $\mathcal{T}_{\text{std}} \subseteq \mathcal{T}_\ell$, (3) test strictness: consider $[a, b) \in \mathcal{T}_\ell$ and point $a$; any standard open interval containing $a$ extends strictly to the left $(a-\epsilon, a+\epsilon) \not\subseteq [a, b)$, (4) conclude $[a, b) \notin \mathcal{T}_{\text{std}}$, so $\mathcal{T}_{\text{std}} \subsetneq \mathcal{T}_\ell$ (the Lower Limit topology is strictly finer).
3. **Slide 3 (`fill_in_blank`):** Single sentence blank testing recall that the lower limit topology is strictly finer than the standard Euclidean topology. Plain text only.
4. **Slide 4 (`numerical`):** Ask student to compute the total number of open sets in the topology on a 3-element set $X = \{a, b, c\}$ generated by the basis $\mathcal{B} = \{\{a\}, \{b, c\}\}$ (open sets: $\emptyset, \{a\}, \{b, c\}, X \implies 4.0$).
5. **Slide 5 (`quiz`):** Quiz asking: Is the Sorgenfrey line $\mathbb{R}_\ell$ second-countable? (No, because any basis must contain a distinct element for each real number $x$, requiring an uncountable basis, even though it is first-countable and separable).
