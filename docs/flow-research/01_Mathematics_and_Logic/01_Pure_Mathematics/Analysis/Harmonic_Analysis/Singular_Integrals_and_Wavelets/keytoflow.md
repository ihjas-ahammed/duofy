## 📌 Core Concept & Mental Model
**Singular Integrals** study operators with non-integrable singular kernels $K(x) \sim \frac{1}{|x|^n}$ that converge only due to **cancellation symmetry**:
* **The Prototypical Example (Hilbert Transform on $\mathbb{R}$):**
  $$Hf(x) = \frac{1}{\pi} \text{p.v.} \int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{f(y)}{x - y}\,dy \iff \widehat{Hf}(\xi) = -i \operatorname{sgn}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)$$
* **Calderón-Zygmund (CZ) Theory:** An operator $T$ bounded on $L^2$ with kernel $K(x, y)$ satisfying size, smoothness, and cancellation conditions is of **weak-type $(1, 1)$** and bounded on $L^p(\mathbb{R}^n)$ for all $1 < p < \infty$ (Marcinkiewicz interpolation).
* **Wavelets & Multiresolution Analysis (MRA):** Orthonormal bases of $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ generated by translations and dyadic dilations of a single mother wavelet $\psi(x)$:
  $$\psi_{j, k}(x) = 2^{j/2}\psi(2^j x - k), \quad j, k \in \mathbb{Z}$$
  achieving simultaneous time and frequency localization (Heisenberg box efficiency).

## 🔑 Best Known Study Methodologies & Learning Framework

### 1. Calderón-Zygmund Decomposition Strategy
Given $f \in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and height $\alpha > 0$, decompose $f = g + b$ where:
1. **The "Good" Part $g(x)$:** Bounded ($|g(x)| \le C \alpha$ a.e.) and $\|g\|_{L^1} \le \|f\|_{L^1}$.
2. **The "Bad" Part $b(x) = \sum_k b_k(x)$:** Supported on disjoint dyadic cubes $Q_k$ with $\sum |Q_k| \le \frac{C}{\alpha}\|f\|_{L^1}$, satisfying the **zero-mean cancellation condition**:
   $$\int_{Q_k} b_k(x)\,dx = 0$$

### 2. End-Point Spaces: $H^1$ and BMO
* At $p=1$, singular integrals fail to map $L^1 \to L^1$; they map into **weak-$L^1$** ($L^{1, \infty}$) and from the **Hardy Space $H^1$** into $L^1$.
* At $p=\infty$, singular integrals map $L^\infty$ into **Bounded Mean Oscillation (BMO)**:
  $$\|f\|_{\text{BMO}} = \sup_Q \frac{1}{|Q|}\int_Q |f(x) - f_Q|\,dx < \infty$$
  (Fefferman's Duality: $(H^1)^* \cong \text{BMO}$).

### 3. Multiresolution Analysis (MRA) Axioms
An increasing sequence of closed subspaces $\dots \subset V_{-1} \subset V_0 \subset V_1 \subset V_2 \subset \dots \subset L^2(\mathbb{R})$ such that:
1. $\overline{\bigcup V_j} = L^2(\mathbb{R})$ and $\bigcap V_j = \{0\}$.
2. $f(x) \in V_j \iff f(2x) \in V_{j+1}$.
3. There exists a scaling function $\phi(x) \in V_0$ whose integer shifts $\{\phi(x - k)\}$ form an orthonormal basis of $V_0$.
4. Wavelet space $W_j = V_{j+1} \ominus V_j$ is spanned by $\{\psi(2^j x - k)\}$.
