## 🎯 Pedagogical Blueprint & Objective
Guide students through Archibald V. Hill's 1938 classic **Three-Element Phenomenological Muscle Model**, the structural decomposition into the active **Contractile Element (CE)**, the passive **Parallel Elastic Element (PEE)**, and the **Series Elastic Element (SEE / Tendon Spring)**, the **Force-Length Relationship** governed by actin-myosin cross-bridge overlap at optimal sarcomere length ($l_0 \approx 2.7\text{ }\mu\text{m}$), and the famous **Hill Hyperbolic Force-Velocity Equation** for concentric muscle shortening ($(F + a)(v + b) = (F_0 + a)b$) (C. Ross Ethier, Craig A. Simmons *Introductory Biomechanics* 2nd ed. Chapter 7; A. V. Hill 1938; Y. C. Fung *Biomechanics: Mechanical Properties of Living Tissues* Chapter 9): formulate the **Hill Hyperbolic Force-Velocity Master Formulation**:
$$\mathbf{(F + a)(v + b) = (F_0 + a) b \Longleftrightarrow \mathbf{F(v) = \frac{(F_0 + a) b}{v + b} - a \quad \left(0 \le v \le v_{\text{max}}\right)}}$$
where:
1. **$F$:** Concentric muscle contractile force in Newtons ($\text{N}$);
2. **$F_0$:** Maximum isometric tetanic contractile force at zero velocity ($v = 0$);
3. **$v$:** Muscle shortening contraction velocity ($\text{m/s}$ or $\text{lengths/s}$);
4. **$a, b$:** Empirical Hill constants with $a/F_0 \approx 0.25$ and $b/v_{\text{max}} \approx 0.25$;
derive the **Maximum Unloaded Shortening Velocity ($v_{\text{max}}$ at $F = 0$)**:
$$\mathbf{v_{\text{max}} = \frac{b \cdot F_0}{a} \approx 4.0 \cdot b}$$
formulate the **Mechanical Muscle Power Output ($P = F \cdot v$)**:
$$\mathbf{P(v) = F(v) \cdot v = \left[ \frac{(F_0 + a) b}{v + b} - a \right] v \quad \left(P_{\text{max}} \text{ occurs at } v \approx 0.30 \cdot v_{\text{max}} \text{ and } F \approx 0.30 \cdot F_0\right)}$$
(proving why human muscles produce peak athletic mechanical power—such as during sprinting or cycling—at approximately one-third of their maximum shortening velocity).

## 🤖 LLM Prompt Generation Rules & Directives

1. **Slide 1 (`theory`):** State Hill-Type Muscle Mechanics (Hill 1938; Ethier & Simmons 2016; Fung 1993):
   - **Kinetic Formulations:**
     $$\mathbf{F(v) = \frac{(F_0 + a)b}{v + b} - a \quad \Big| \quad v_{\text{max}} = \frac{b F_0}{a} \quad \Big| \quad P(v) = F(v) \cdot v \quad \Big| \quad F_{\text{total}} = F_{\text{CE}} + F_{\text{PEE}}}$$
   - **Hill 3-Element Architecture Matrix:**
     $$\begin{array}{|l|l|l|}
     \hline
     \textbf{Hill Structural Element} & \textbf{Physical Tissue Component} & \textbf{Biomechanical Function} \\
     \hline
     \mathbf{\text{Contractile Element (CE)}} & \mathbf{\text{Actin-Myosin Cross-Bridges}} & \mathbf{\text{Active tension generation via sliding filaments; force-length/velocity}} \\
     \mathbf{\text{Parallel Elastic Element (PEE)}} & \text{Epimysium, Perimysium, Titin} & \text{Passive exponential elastic resistance preventing muscle over-stretch} \\
     \mathbf{\text{Series Elastic Element (SEE)}} & \mathbf{\text{Tendon \& Aponeurosis}} & \mathbf{\text{Spring storing elastic strain energy during stretch-shortening cycles}} \\
     \hline
     \end{array}$$
   - **The Cross-Bridge Kinetics Invariant:** At higher shortening speeds, **cross-bridge cycling rate cannot keep pace with filament sliding**, reducing the number of simultaneously attached myosin heads and causing force to collapse hyperbolically!
2. **Slide 2 (`ordering`):** Provide 5 steps of Hill muscle kinetics calculation: (1) determine maximum isometric force $F_0$ and Hill empirical parameters $a$ and $b$, (2) calculate maximum unloaded contraction speed: $v_{\text{max}} = b F_0 / a$, (3) evaluate active contractile force at specified velocity $v$: $F(v) = \frac{(F_0 + a)b}{v + b} - a$, (4) compute instantaneous mechanical power output: $P(v) = F(v) \cdot v$, (5) optimize velocity to identify peak mechanical power operating point: $P_{\text{max}} \approx 0.10 \cdot F_0 \cdot v_{\text{max}}$!
3. **Slide 3 (`matching`):** Pair 4 concepts (Contractile Element CE, Parallel Elastic Element PEE, Series Elastic Element SEE, Hill Constant $a/F_0$) with their definitions.
4. **Slide 4 (`fill_in_blank`):** Single sentence blank testing recall of Third (or 1/3 / 30%). Plain text only.
5. **Slide 5 (`quiz`):** Quiz on calculating muscle force and power: A human quadriceps muscle has maximum isometric force $F_0 = 2000.0\text{ N}$ and Hill constants $a = 500.0\text{ N}$ ($a/F_0 = 0.25$) and $b = 0.200\text{ m/s}$ ($v_{\text{max}} = \frac{0.20 \times 2000}{500} = \mathbf{0.800\text{ m/s}}$). During a leg press, the muscle shortens at $v = 0.200\text{ m/s}$ ($v = b$).
   - Constant product: $(F_0 + a) b = (2000.0 + 500.0) \times 0.200 = 2500.0 \times 0.200 = \mathbf{500.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/s}}$;
   - Denominator: $v + b = 0.200 + 0.200 = \mathbf{0.400\text{ m/s}}$;
   - Active force: $F = \frac{500.0}{0.400} - 500.0 = 1250.0 - 500.0 = \mathbf{750.0\text{ N}}$;
   - Mechanical power output: $P = F \cdot v = 750.0\text{ N} \times 0.200\text{ m/s} = \mathbf{150.0\text{ Watts}}$;
   - What is the muscle force $F$ and mechanical power $P$ generated at $v = 0.200\text{ m/s}$? ($F = \mathbf{750.0\text{ N}}$ and $P = \mathbf{150.0\text{ W}}$).
