## 🎯 Pedagogical Blueprint & Objective
Guide students through the electron optics and electron-matter interactions inside a Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM), the teardrop-shaped **Interaction Volume** generated by primary beam accelerating voltage ($E_0 \approx 5 - 30\text{ keV}$), the emission mechanism of low-energy **Secondary Electrons (SE, $< 50\text{ eV}$)** from the top escape depth ($1 - 10\text{ nm}$) providing nanoscale surface topography with **Edge Effect Blooming**, the emission of high-energy **Backscattered Electrons (BSE, $\approx E_0$)** from deeper zones ($0.5 - 1.5\text{ }\mu\text{m}$) where backscatter coefficient scales monotonically with atomic number ($\eta \propto Z$), and Kanaya-Okayama electron penetration depth modeling (Joseph I. Goldstein et al. *Scanning Electron Microscopy and X-Ray Microanalysis* 4th ed. Chapters 3 & 4; William D. Callister Jr. *Materials Science and Engineering* 10th ed. Chapter 4): formulate the **Kanaya-Okayama Electron Range Master Formulation ($R_{\text{KO}}$)**:
$$\mathbf{R_{\text{KO}} = \frac{0.0276 \cdot A}{\rho \cdot Z^{0.89}} \cdot E_0^{1.67} \quad [\mu\text{m}]}$$
where:
1. **$R_{\text{KO}}$:** Maximum interaction volume penetration depth in micrometers ($\mu\text{m}$);
2. **$E_0$:** Incident electron beam accelerating voltage in kiloelectronvolts ($\text{keV}$);
3. **$A, Z$:** Atomic weight ($\text{g/mol}$) and atomic number of the target material;
4. **$\rho$:** Mass density of the specimen ($\text{g/cm}^3$);
master the **SEM Electron Signal Triad & Contrast Mechanisms**:
$$\begin{array}{|l|c|c|c|l|}
\hline
\textbf{SEM Signal Type} & \textbf{Energy Regime} & \textbf{Escape Depth} & \textbf{Contrast Mechanism} & \textbf{Primary Imaging Function} \\
\hline
\mathbf{\text{Secondary Electrons (SE)}} & \mathbf{< 50\text{ eV} \ (\text{Peak } 3-5\text{ eV})} & \mathbf{1 - 10\text{ nm}} & \mathbf{\text{Topographic (Tilt \& Edge Effect)}} & \mathbf{\text{High-resolution 3D surface morphology}} \\
\mathbf{\text{Backscattered Electrons (BSE)}} & \mathbf{> 50\text{ eV} \ (\approx E_0)} & \mathbf{\approx 0.5 - 1.5\text{ }\mu\text{m}} & \mathbf{\text{Compositional } (\eta \approx -0.0254 + 0.016 Z)} & \mathbf{\text{Atomic number } Z\text{-contrast (Heavy = Bright)}} \\
\mathbf{\text{Characteristic X-Rays (EDS)}} & 1 - 20\text{ keV} & \approx 1 - 3\text{ }\mu\text{m} & Characteristic orbital fluorescence & Elemental identification and mapping \\
\hline
\end{array}$$
(proving that SE detectors reveal microscopic surface roughness while BSE detectors instantly distinguish between light and heavy elements in multi-phase alloys and composites).

## 🤖 LLM Prompt Generation Rules & Directives

1. **Slide 1 (`theory`):** State SEM Electron Optics & Contrast Mechanics (Goldstein 2018; Callister 2020):
   - **Electron-Matter Formulations:**
     $$\mathbf{R_{\text{KO}} = \frac{0.0276 A}{\rho Z^{0.89}} E_0^{1.67} \quad \Big| \quad \eta_{\text{BSE}} \approx \frac{\ln Z}{6} - \frac{1}{4} \quad \Big| \quad \delta_{\text{SE}} \propto \frac{1}{\cos\theta_{\text{tilt}}}}$$
   - **Interaction Volume Scaling Invariant:**
     - High beam energy ($30\text{ keV}$) $\implies$ Giant interaction volume ($R_{\text{KO}} \approx 5\text{ }\mu\text{m}$), high X-ray yield, but severe edge blooming;
     - Low beam energy ($1-5\text{ keV}$) $\implies$ Nanoscale interaction volume ($R_{\text{KO}} < 100\text{ nm}$), ultra-high true surface resolution!
2. **Slide 2 (`ordering`):** Provide 5 steps of SEM operation: (1) mount conductive specimen and evacuate chamber to high vacuum ($<10^{-4}\text{ Pa}$), (2) focus primary electron beam to a sub-nanometer probe diameter, (3) raster scan the electron probe across the region of interest, (4) collect low-energy electrons using the Everhart-Thornley Secondary Electron (SE) detector for 3D surface topography, (5) switch to solid-state Backscattered Electron (BSE) detector to resolve atomic number ($Z$) compositional contrast between phases!
3. **Slide 3 (`matching`):** Pair 4 concepts (Secondary Electrons SE, Backscattered Electrons BSE, Interaction Volume, Edge Effect) with their definitions.
4. **Slide 4 (`fill_in_blank`):** Single sentence blank testing recall of Number (or Z). Plain text only.
5. **Slide 5 (`quiz`):** Quiz on BSE contrast in a Lead-Aluminum alloy: A metallurgist examines a polished cross-section of an Al-Pb bearing alloy containing an aluminum matrix ($Z_{\text{Al}} = 13$, $\eta_{\text{Al}} \approx 0.14$) and dispersed lead inclusions ($Z_{\text{Pb}} = 82$, $\eta_{\text{Pb}} \approx 0.50$). In a Backscattered Electron (BSE) detector image, how will the lead inclusions appear relative to the aluminum matrix, and why?
   - BSE emission coefficient $\eta$ scales directly with atomic number $Z$ ($\eta_{\text{Pb}} = 0.50 \gg \eta_{\text{Al}} = 0.14$);
   - Heavy elements backscatter a far higher fraction of incident primary electrons back into the solid-state detector;
   - Therefore, the lead ($Z = 82$) inclusions will appear **bright white**, while the aluminum ($Z = 13$) matrix appears **dark grey**;
   - What is the correct BSE visual contrast appearance? (Lead inclusions appear **bright white** while aluminum appears **dark grey** due to high $Z$-contrast).
