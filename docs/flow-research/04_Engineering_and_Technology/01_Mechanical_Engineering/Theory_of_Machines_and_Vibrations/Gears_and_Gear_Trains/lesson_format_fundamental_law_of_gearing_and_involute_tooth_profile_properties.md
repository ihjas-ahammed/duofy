## 🎯 Pedagogical Blueprint & Objective
Guide students through conjugate tooth action, the fundamental theorem of gearing, and involute geometry in power transmission engineering (Joseph E. Shigley et al. *Theory of Machines and Mechanisms* Chapter 7; Richard G. Budynas & J. Keith Nisbett *Shigley's Mechanical Engineering Design* Chapter 13): master the **Fundamental Law of Gearing**:
$$\mathbf{\frac{\omega_1}{\omega_2} = \frac{r_{p2}}{r_{p1}} = \frac{d_2}{d_1} = \frac{T_2}{T_1} = \text{Constant}}$$
(stating that to maintain a strictly constant angular velocity ratio, the **common normal to the tooth profiles at the point of contact MUST always pass through a fixed point on the line of centers, called the Pitch Point $P$**), master **Involute Curve Geometry** generated by unwrapping a taut string from a **Base Circle ($\mathbf{r_b = r_p \cos\phi}$)** where $\phi$ is the pressure angle (typically $20^\circ$ standard), and prove the **Center Distance Insensitivity Theorem** (demonstrating why involute gears are universally used across modern industry: if manufacturing tolerances or thermal expansion alter the center distance between shafts, the operating pressure angle $\phi$ shifts slightly, but **the angular velocity ratio $\mathbf{\frac{\omega_1}{\omega_2}}$ remains 100% CONSTANT and conjugate**!).

## 🤖 LLM Prompt Generation Rules & Directives

1. **Slide 1 (`theory`):** State Fundamental Law of Gearing & Involute (Shigley 2014; Budynas 2020):
   - **Fundamental Law Formulation:**
     $$\mathbf{\frac{\omega_1}{\omega_2} = \frac{r_{p2}}{r_{p1}} = \frac{T_2}{T_1} = \text{Constant} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \mathbf{r_b = r_p \cos\phi}}$$
   - **Gear Nomenclature Matrix:**
     $$\begin{array}{|l|l|l|l|}
     \hline
     \textbf{Gear Feature} & \textbf{Symbol} & \textbf{Formula} & \textbf{Physical Definition} \\
     \hline
     \mathbf{\text{Module}} & \mathbf{m} & \mathbf{m = \frac{d}{T}} \ [\text{mm/tooth}] & \text{Standard metric tooth size metric} \\
     \mathbf{\text{Pitch Circle Diameter}} & \mathbf{d} & \mathbf{d = m \cdot T} & \text{Imaginary friction rolling circle diameter} \\
     \mathbf{\text{Base Circle Diameter}} & \mathbf{d_b} & \mathbf{d_b = d \cos\phi} & \text{Circle from which involute curve is generated} \\
     \mathbf{\text{Circular Pitch}} & \mathbf{p_c} & \mathbf{p_c = \pi m = \frac{\pi d}{T}} & \text{Distance along pitch circle between adjacent teeth} \\
     \mathbf{\text{Base Pitch}} & \mathbf{p_b} & \mathbf{p_b = p_c \cos\phi = \pi m \cos\phi} & \text{Distance between adjacent teeth along line of action} \\
     \hline
     \end{array}$$
   - **Center Distance Invariant:** Changing shaft center distance alters the operating pitch circles and pressure angle $\phi'$, but because base circles $r_{b1}, r_{b2}$ are fixed, the velocity ratio $\frac{\omega_1}{\omega_2} = \frac{r_{b2}}{r_{b1}}$ **remains completely constant**!
2. **Slide 2 (`ordering`):** Provide 5 steps of gear geometric design: (1) determine required gear ratio $i = \frac{T_2}{T_1}$ and select standard module $m$ (e.g. $m = 4\text{ mm}$), (2) compute pitch circle diameters $d_1 = m T_1$ and $d_2 = m T_2$, (3) calculate base circle radii using $r_b = r_p \cos\phi$ with pressure angle $\phi = 20^\circ$, (4) compute standard addendum $a = 1.0 m$ and dedendum $b = 1.25 m$ to establish tip circle $r_a = r_p + a$ and root circle $r_d = r_p - b$, (5) calculate base pitch $p_b = \pi m \cos\phi$ and circular pitch $p_c = \pi m$!
3. **Slide 3 (`matching`):** Pair 4 concepts (Pitch Point $P$, Base Circle $r_b$, Module $m$, Base Pitch $p_b$) with their definitions.
4. **Slide 4 (`fill_in_blank`):** Single sentence blank testing recall of Pitch. Plain text only.
5. **Slide 5 (`quiz`):** Quiz on center distance insensitivity: In industrial machinery manufacturing, why are Involute tooth profiles universally favored over Cycloidal tooth profiles for spur and helical gears? (The involute curve is generated from a fixed base circle $r_b = r_p \cos\phi$; **if the center distance between the two gear shafts shifts slightly due to assembly tolerances, bearing wear, or thermal expansion, the line of action remains the common tangent to the two base circles; although the operating pressure angle $\phi$ changes slightly, the velocity ratio $\frac{\omega_1}{\omega_2} = \frac{r_{b2}}{r_{b1}}$ remains strictly constant and conjugate, eliminating velocity fluctuations and excessive vibration, whereas cycloidal gears require exact center distances to maintain a constant speed ratio**).
