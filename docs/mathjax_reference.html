Section 1: Fractions, Radicals & Algebraic Foundations

Key Formulas & Notation
  • Inline fractions: $\frac{a}{b}$ and $\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}$ vs display fractions $\displaystyle \frac{a+b}{c+d}$.
  • Radicals and roots: $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$, $\sqrt[n]{\frac{x}{y}}$, and nested radicals $\sqrt{1 + \sqrt{1 + x}}$.
  • Binomial coefficients and combinatorial sums: $\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}$.

In standard university algebra, rational expressions and algebraic identities are represented with exact fractional syntax:

Theorem 1.1 (Quadratic Formula & Roots) Theorem

For any polynomial equation $a x^2 + b x + c = 0$ with $a, b, c \in \mathbb{R}$ and $a \neq 0$, the roots are given by:

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$
Proof (Completing the Square).

Divide by $a$ to obtain $x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x + \frac{c}{a} = 0$. Add $\left(\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2$ to both sides:

$$\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}$$

Taking square roots yields $x + \frac{b}{2a} = \pm \frac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$, proving the claim. $\blacksquare$

Section 2: Greek Letters, Number Sets & Mathematical Logic

Pure mathematics relies on standard Blackboard Bold sets and formal logical quantifiers:

$$\mathbb{N} \subset \mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{R} \subset \mathbb{C}$$
Definition 2.1 (Universal Quantification & Continuous Functions) Definition

A function $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ is continuous at $x_0$ if and only if:

$$\forall \varepsilon > 0, \; \exists \delta > 0 \quad \text{such that} \quad 0 < |x - x_0| < \delta \implies |f(x) - f(x_0)| < \varepsilon$$
Significance & Structural Analysis.

This $(\varepsilon, \delta)$-formulation establishes the topological foundation for analysis without relying on infinitesimal quantities. $\blacksquare$

Logic & Set Theory Pitfall

Always distinguish proper inclusion $A \subset B$ from generalized inclusion $A \subseteq B$, and note that $\emptyset \neq \{\emptyset\}$.

Section 3: Calculus, Differential Operators, Integrals & Series

Calculus operations encompass ordinary derivatives, partial derivatives, iterated integrals, and summation limits:

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(x)}{x} = 1, \qquad \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}, \qquad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^2} \, dx = \sqrt{\pi}$$
Theorem 3.1 (Fundamental Theorem of Calculus & Stokes' Theorem) Theorem

If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and $F'(x) = f(x)$, then:

$$\int_{a}^{b} f(t) \, dt = F(b) - F(a)$$

In higher dimensions, Stokes' theorem equates the boundary integral to the interior differential form:

$$\oint_{\partial \Sigma} \mathbf{A} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_{\Sigma} (\nabla \times \mathbf{A}) \cdot d\mathbf{S}$$
Proof Sketch.

Partition $[a, b]$ into $n$ subintervals with $t_i = a + i\Delta t$. By the Mean Value Theorem, $F(t_i) - F(t_{i-1}) = f(c_i)\Delta t$. Summing over $i=1 \dots n$ and taking $n \to \infty$ gives the definite integral. $\blacksquare$

Section 4: Linear Algebra, Matrices & Systems of Equations

Linear mappings and matrix representations use standard bracketed and parenthesized environments:

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} & \dots & a_{1n} \\ a_{21} & a_{22} & \dots & a_{2n} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ a_{m1} & a_{m2} & \dots & a_{mn} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \det(A) = \begin{vmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{vmatrix} = ad - bc$$
Theorem 4.1 (Eigenvalue Characteristic Equation) Theorem

For a square matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$, a scalar $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue with eigenvector $\mathbf{v} \neq \mathbf{0}$ if and only if:

$$\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$$
Proof.

$A\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v} \iff (A - \lambda I)\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$. A non-trivial solution $\mathbf{v} \neq \mathbf{0}$ exists if and only if the nullspace is non-zero, requiring $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$. $\blacksquare$

Section 6: Worked Examples & Evaluation

Example 6.1: Definite Integral Evaluation

Worked Solution

Compute $\displaystyle \int_{0}^{1} x e^x \, dx$.

Using integration by parts with $u = x$ and $dv = e^x dx$:

$$\int x e^x dx = x e^x - \int e^x dx = (x - 1)e^x$$

Evaluating from $0$ to $1$:

$$\left[(1 - 1)e^1\right] - \left[(0 - 1)e^0\right] = 0 - (-1) = 1$$

Example 6.2: Matrix Determinant & Invertibility

Worked Solution

Given $M = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 4 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$, find $M^{-1}$.

$$\det(M) = (2)(3) - (1)(4) = 6 - 4 = 2 \neq 0$$ $$M^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -4 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.5 & -0.5 \\ -2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
